"Connect AI to our 1C" is one of the most common requests that businesses from the CIS come to us with. Behind it there is usually a specific kind of fatigue: a manager moves orders from a messenger into the accounting system by hand, the accountant types in supplier invoices, the owner asks for a sales export and waits half a day for it. It seems that AI should simply take this over and do it.

In part, that is true. But 1C and other accounting systems are not a CRM with an open API and one page of documentation. They have their own logic, their own limitations and their own ways of breaking everything. Let's look at which tasks are solved reliably, which come with caveats, and which are better left alone, as well as what to check before the project starts.

First, a distinction: AI and integration are two different tasks

When people say "integrating AI with 1C," they mix two layers.

The first layer is data exchange. How an external system reads stock levels, prices and counterparties from 1C and writes orders, documents and payments into it. This is a classic integration task, and AI plays no part in it. This is the territory of 1C HTTP services, OData, file exchange, and ready-made connectors in n8n or Make.

The second layer is intelligent processing. A language model understands a customer's request in natural language, recognizes an invoice from a photo, and composes an answer to the owner's question "how much did we sell in Tbilisi last month." This layer works with data already retrieved from 1C and prepares data that needs to be written into 1C.

Most of the problems in such projects are in the first layer. The model can be as smart as you like, but if 1C has no external access or the product catalog is kept haphazardly, the result will be poor. So a project should be assessed starting from the state of the accounting system, not from the choice of model.

Which scenarios work reliably

Answering questions about accounting data

The owner writes in Telegram: "What is the week's revenue across the three stores?" The agent turns the question into a query to 1C, gets the data and replies with text or a table. Reading from the accounting system is a safe operation: nothing breaks, and at worst the answer will be incomplete. This is a good first project, and it is also the basis for automatic summaries in Telegram.

A caveat: the quality of the answers comes down to the quality of the data. If the same product is entered in the catalog three times under different names, the agent will faithfully count three products.

Checking stock and prices for customer channels

A customer asks about availability and price in a messenger — the agent looks in 1C and answers. The technical design matters here: don't query 1C for every question, but keep an intermediate copy of stock levels that is updated every few minutes. Direct queries to the production database from an external channel create load, and in peak season they can slow down the work of cashiers and managers.

Recognizing incoming documents with subsequent review

Supplier invoices, delivery notes and acceptance certificates arrive by email as PDFs or photos. The model extracts the fields — supplier, number, date, line items, amounts — and prepares a draft document. The accountant reviews and posts it. The key word is "draft": we don't recommend automatic posting of documents without review in any project. More on such scenarios in the article on AI for document workflows.

Creating orders from a messenger with confirmation

The agent takes an order in Telegram or WhatsApp, clarifies the items and quantities, matches them against the product catalog and creates a customer order in 1C with the status "requires confirmation." The manager opens it, checks it and posts it. If the catalog is clean and customers name products close to how they are recorded, the matching works well. If customers write "that blue one we got last time," the agent needs the counterparty's order history, and that is already a more complex integration.

What works with caveats

  • Non-standard configurations. Many companies have had their 1C modified over the years by different programmers. Standard connectors may not know about the added fields, and part of the integration will have to be written on the 1C side. That is normal, but it extends the timeline and requires a 1C specialist, which an AI contractor may not have.
  • On-premise versions and the cloud. 1C installed on a server in the office without external access requires setting up a secure channel or an intermediate service that reaches the database from inside the network. Cloud options are usually simpler but depend on the plan and the provider.
  • Other accounting systems. Businesses in Georgia or Europe often have not 1C but local accounting services, mid-range ERP systems or simply Excel. The principles are the same: is there an API, who is responsible for it, how clean is the data. For systems without an API, what remains is scheduled file export — slower, but it works.
  • Writing financial documents. Payments, cash operations, adjustments — technically possible, but an AI error here is costly, and correcting posted documents in the books takes the accountant's time. At AiKrok we leave such operations to people and give the agent the preparation of drafts.

What is better left alone

There are tasks that sound attractive but in practice lead to lost time or money.

"Let AI figure out our database by itself." If the product catalog, counterparties and contracts are kept chaotically, AI will not bring order. It will work confidently with dirty data and give confident wrong answers. Cleaning up the reference data comes first — that is work for an accounting specialist, not for a model.

Automatic period closing, tax calculation, payroll. Statutory accounting is an area where a mistake turns into fines and audits. Automation through data exchange under clear rules is appropriate here, but not an agent with the right to make decisions.

Replacing 1C with a "smart agent." Sometimes people ask for an agent to "keep the books in a chat" instead of the system. Accounting is data integrity, history, reporting. An agent is good as an interface to the accounting system, but not as its replacement.

What a typical architecture looks like

For small and medium businesses, a working design usually includes four elements:

  1. An access point to 1C — a published HTTP service or OData on the accounting system side, with a separate user and limited permissions: read what is needed, write only to permitted documents.
  2. An intermediate layer — n8n, Make or a small service that stores a cache of stock levels and prices, queues tasks, logs all operations and keeps the model from touching the database directly.
  3. A language model — a cloud one (GPT-5, Claude, Gemini) or one deployed on your own server (Qwen, Llama, Mistral) if the data can't be sent outside. For accounting data the choice is a pressing question — see the article on data security when implementing AI.
  4. A channel — a Telegram bot, WhatsApp, a web widget or an internal chat for employees.

An important detail: the model never composes database queries as "free text." It chooses from predefined operations with validated parameters: "get the stock level by SKU," "create an order with these items." This limits what it can do but protects the books from surprises.

What to check before the project starts

It is worth going through this list before discussing timelines and budget with a contractor.

  • Which version and configuration of 1C you have, how heavily it has been modified, and whether you have an in-house 1C programmer or one you know.
  • Where the database is hosted — on-premise, with a hosting provider, in the cloud — and whether external access to it can be published.
  • Who will issue a technical user with limited permissions and who is responsible for this on the company's side.
  • How clean the reference data is: how many duplicates are in the product catalog, whether SKUs are filled in, whether prices are up to date.
  • Which operations the agent is allowed to perform by itself, which require confirmation, and which are prohibited. This is the basis of the technical specification for the agent.
  • Whether data can be sent to a cloud model or a local one is needed.

Summary

AI gets along well with 1C and accounting systems where it reads data and answers people, prepares drafts of documents and orders, and processes incoming paperwork. It is a poor fit for posting financial operations independently, and it is no substitute for order in the reference data.

Start with reading: summaries for the owner or stock checks in a customer channel. This is safe, pays back quickly and, along the way, shows the real state of the data in the books. Writing documents is added as a second stage, once the integration and data quality have been verified.
